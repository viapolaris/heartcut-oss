# The Last Light

An old lighthouse keeper, about 78, thin, with a grey beard and a dark blue wool jumper with a hole at the left elbow. He has kept this light for 40 years. His cat is a big orange tabby, 9 years old, about 6 kg, with a torn left ear and a white tip on her tail.

Tomorrow a machine takes over the light and he has to leave. Tonight is his last night.

He climbs the spiral stairs slowly, one hand on the rail. The cat goes ahead and waits on each landing. At the top he cleans the big lens with a cloth, the way he has done every night, even though it does not matter any more. He winds the old clock. The beam starts to turn over a dark, calm sea.

He sits on a wooden stool by the window with the cat on his knees. In a short flashback he is young, about 35, dark beard, the same jumper but new, carrying a tiny orange kitten up the same stairs in his coat pocket.

Back in the present, the first grey light of morning comes. He switches the lamp off by hand for the last time. The cat jumps down and walks to the door, then looks back at him. He takes his cap off the hook and follows her down.

It should feel quiet and tender, not sad. Small movements. No one speaks.
