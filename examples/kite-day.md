# Kite Day

A girl, 8 years old, small for her age, with two short black braids, a yellow raincoat and red rubber boots. Her grandfather is 72, tall and a bit bent, with white hair, round glasses and a brown corduroy jacket with leather patches on the elbows.

They make a kite together at his kitchen table out of newspaper, two sticks and string. It is crooked. She is worried it will not fly. He tells her it does not need to be pretty, it needs to be light.

They walk up a green hill on a windy afternoon, the grass bending. The first try fails and the kite drops nose first. She is close to tears. He kneels down to her height, ties a longer tail on the kite from his own red scarf, and hands her the string.

On the second try the kite climbs high. She runs, laughing. He stands still with his hands in his pockets and watches her, not the kite.

At the end they sit on the hill. She leans on his arm. The kite is still up there, a small dot against the clouds.

Warm, light, a little funny. Real wind in the hair and the grass.
